The Topology
of Cyclic Capture.

Unlike chess where piece superiority is transitive and scalar (Queen > Rook > Bishop > Pawn), JANKEN operates on the intransitive cyclic ring $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$: $R \succ S \succ P \succ R$. This circular dominance turns evaluation into a topological problem of cycles, attractors, and Strongly Connected Components (SCCs).

This laboratory inspects how variant capture rules warp the state space digraph, why games decompose into a DAG of material strata, and how reachability bounding tames large boards.

Material Strata & The Macro DAG

Why the overall state space is an acyclic cascade of cyclic pools.

Every capture strictly decreases total surviving material $M = |B| + |R|$. Because captures cannot be reversed (no promotions, no re-spawns), no move can ever return to a higher material stratum. The state space graph is therefore a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) of material layers.

The Azel's Wall Reachability Frontier

Azel's Wall is a 5×5 asymmetric layout ($2R / 1P / 3S$). While the full 5×5 state space contains $\approx 3.58 \times 10^{14}$ theoretical states, forward reachability from Azel's fixed starting line traverses only $\sim 4.5\%$ of the placements.

Graph-Theoretic Invariants

Attractors, Repetition Cycles, and Terminal Sinks

In retrograde analysis, positions resolve into three fundamental topological categories:

Terminal Sinks

Positions where the game ends by rule: elimination of the opponent's army or arrival at zero legal moves. These are the base cases from which retrograde solving propagates backwards.

Attractor Cycles (SCCs)

Subgraphs where neither player can force an escape into a won terminal state. Moves cycle indefinitely, forming the draw-by-threefold attractor basins that characterise perfect play in JANKEN.

Transient Strata Cascades

Directed pathways where every edge moves strictly downward in distance-to-mate (DTM) or down material layers, funneling non-cyclically toward a terminal conclusion.
