The Topology
of Cyclic Capture.

Unlike chess where piece superiority is transitive and scalar (Queen > Rook > Bishop > Pawn), JANKEN operates on the intransitive cyclic ring $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$: $R \succ S \succ P \succ R$. This circular dominance turns evaluation into a topological problem of cycles, attractors, and Strongly Connected Components (SCCs).

This laboratory inspects how variant capture rules warp the state space digraph, why games decompose into a DAG of material strata, and how reachability bounding tames large boards.

Graph-Theoretic Invariants

Attractors, Repetition Cycles, and Terminal Sinks

In retrograde analysis, positions resolve into three fundamental topological categories:

Terminal Sinks

Positions where the game ends by rule: elimination of the opponent's army, deadlocks where no captures are possible, or arrival at zero legal moves. These are the base cases from which retrograde solving propagates backwards.

Attractor Cycles (SCCs)

Subgraphs where neither player can force an escape into a won terminal state. Moves cycle indefinitely, forming the draw-by-threefold attractor basins that characterise perfect play in cyclic games.

Transient Strata Cascades

Directed pathways where every edge moves strictly downward in distance-to-mate (DTM) or down material layers, funneling non-cyclically toward a terminal conclusion.
