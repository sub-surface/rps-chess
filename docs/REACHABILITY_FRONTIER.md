# Forward Reachability & Contact Dynamics

## 1. Reachable Graph vs Unconstrained Placement Universe

A critical insight from tablebase research is the divergence between:
1. **The Combinatorial Placement Universe $\mathcal{U}$:** All mathematically distinct arrangements of piece multisets across the board cells.
2. **The Reachable Subgraph $\mathcal{G}_{\text{reachable}}(s_0)$:** The connected component generated by expanding legal moves forward from a designated starting formation $s_0$.

While $|\mathcal{U}|$ for a 5×5 game is $3.58 \times 10^{14}$ states, forward exploration from symmetric start formations prunes more than **95%** of those placements as physically unreachable under legal move mechanics.

## 2. The Azel's Wall Census Experiment (SPEC §8.6 D13)

Azel's Wall is a fixed 5×5 starting formation ($2R / 1P / 3S$ per side, 12 pieces total):
- **Blue Formation:** Scissors screen in front of Rock / Paper / Rock on files 0 and 1.
- **Red Formation:** 180° rotational reflection across the board center.

We executed a deterministic forward Breadth-First Search (BFS) census ([`scripts/azel-wall.mjs`](../scripts/azel-wall.mjs)) recording states, transitions, captures, and cycle revisits up to a 50,000-state cap.

### 2.2 Key Findings: The Contact Phase Transition

1. **The Neutral Zone Buffer:**
   On Plies 0 and 1, **zero captures** take place. The armies are separated by file 2 (the central column), requiring 2 plies of movement before piece ranges intersect.
2. **First Contact at Ply 2:**
   At ply 2, exactly **6 capture edges** emerge as forward scissors clash across the central file.
3. **Melee and the Attractor Explosion:**
   By plies 3 and 4, captures surge to 373, and the search discovers **40,093 cycle revisits** (re-encountering states already in the visited hash set).
   This confirms that even during the opening contact phase, cyclic dominance causes game trajectories to repeatedly cross their own paths, forming dense Strongly Connected Components (SCCs).
